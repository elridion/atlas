# Changelog

## 0.1.0

- In worlds without a map, "Record discoveries" at a cartography table now also updates your atlas,
  and the map key unrolls it: a hand-inked map in the style of a classic fantasy novel, with
  unexplored lands left as blank parchment. Golden sparkles rise from the table while the atlas
  is being updated and burst once it is done.
- Every biome has a faint colour wash of its own under the ink, so biomes read apart at any zoom,
  and water is one even indigo wash ending crisply at the shore. The colours are thin glazes of pigments a northern illuminator of the
  period could have had.
- The parchment is aged all over: an uneven patina, old water stains with tide lines, dried rings and clusters of small brown spots, showing through the colours as on real stained paper.
- Woods are drawn where the game grows its forests, each one a dense mass of trees bounded by its
  outer crowns, with the open land around it kept clear.
- Every biome has its own pen marks: ranges of shaded peaks ringed by foothills, grass and woods of
  round crowns in the Meadows, stones and dark pine woods in the Black Forest, wavelets, marsh tufts
  and dead trees in the Swamp, a dry stipple and copses on the Plains, mist banks over giant trees
  and rock pillars in the Mistlands, burnt ground, lava cracks and volcanoes in the Ashlands, and a
  near-blank snowfield with ice ridges in the Deep North.
- Coasts are ringed by ripple lines that fade out over open water, with ice floes in the northern
  sea, and rivers and streams are drawn as ink lines.
- "Read map" at a cartography table opens the game's own interactive map, where you can place and
  edit pins. Your pins appear on the atlas the next time you record your discoveries, inked black
  on a flat parchment padding that follows each icon, names outlined in parchment, so they stand clear of the map.
- The atlas is drawn once per world in the background and kept, so opening it never stalls the game.
- Zooming in redraws the part in view at that zoom from the world itself: coasts and wood edges
  follow the land to within a few metres, and woods fill with more, finer trees. Close-ups are drawn
  ahead of time in small tiles around the pointer, so zooming in rarely waits.
